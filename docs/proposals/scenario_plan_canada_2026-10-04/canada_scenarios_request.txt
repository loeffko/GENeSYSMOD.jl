1.	Unleash of Canada’s electricity generation potential [Mixed]: Signal- Double electricity grid capacity by 2050 (from 150 GW to ~310 GW) to support economic growth, energy security, electrification, AI/data center demand, and decarbonization. Maintaining flexibility through a broad energy mix, including natural gas, while advancing clean electricity and emissions reductions. 

2.	Local Content no longer an option [Mixed]: Major utilities, Provinces (ON, AB, QC), industrial customers and public-sector buyers increasingly require evidence of Canadian content, local services, regional partnerships, domestic fabrication or assembly. This will become differentiator in competitive procurements across all sectors. Signal- consistent calls from AIEQ, OPG, Invest Alberta, CANDU for SE to invest in local manufacturing.

3.	Acceleration of Nuclear Growth [Tailwind]: Signal- Canada is entering a series of nuclear growth cycles starting 2030: 22 GW of announced projects, ~ $13B market potential for SE. Enabling CANDU reactors’ position to add 28 new reactors by 2050. Including the expansion of GE Vernova Hitachi SMRs in Ontario, other provinces (SK, NB) and internationally. More project announcements expected on MMR deployment in northern territories to strengthen Arctic defence.

4.	Indigenous equity and partnership: a project-gating factor. [Mixed/Tailwind]
Projects with meaningful Indigenous ownership and benefit-sharing can move faster and gain legitimacy; projects without it may face delay. Signal- Seven Williams Treaties First Nations have secured a historic $700-million equity partnership in the Darlington New Nuclear Project.

5.	Awakening of Northern Territories and remote power systems. [Tailwind]
Mining, defense, critical minerals, and diesel displacement could support microgrids, SMRs, storage, hybrid systems, and resilient power solutions.

6.	Reaching Data Sovereignty  Acceleration of Data Centers: [Tailwind] Signal: TC Energy is developing a concept for up to three gas-fired power plants at its Crossfield, Alberta site, with each plant targeting approximately 465 MW, for a potential total capacity of around 1.4 GW.
7.	Wind market re-acceleration [Mixed/Tailwind]: Signal- Quebec is the anchor market, with Hydro-Québec targeting up to 10 GW of wind by 2035, while Ontario, B.C., Manitoba, Saskatchewan and Atlantic Canada add smaller but material procurement waves. The opportunity is strongest where wind is paired with transmission expansion, storage, Indigenous equity partnerships, and grid-stability solutions. SE implication: Key headwinds remain product adaptation for cold climate/de-icing, tower/tariff exposure, and competitive pressure.

FID faster. 26 new investments/partnerships with allied countries. 221 active exploration licenses, including $1.1B committed investment.

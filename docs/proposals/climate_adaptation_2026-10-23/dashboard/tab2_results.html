A hotter grid: what today's plan risks, and what planning for it costs

Three climate cases on top of the FEL26 Base: Air temp increase (hotter summers: more cooling, less heating, thermal plants untouched), Severe (plus a drought year: cooling-water limits on the steam plants) and Extreme (plus a historic heat wave: the hardest thermal derates). Select a year above; 2035 is the last year with climate data.

1 · Today's plan in a hotter climate

The FEL26 Base fleet, as planned without climate inputs, dispatched hour by hour under each climate case. Fleets that were planned with the climate inputs have no shortfall in any year and are left out here; they appear in sections 2 and 3.

2 · Planning for it: how the fleet changes

The same cases as investment runs: the model may build what it needs. It answers hotter summers with gas turbines and batteries first, keeps coal online longer under thermal stress, and adds solar for the summer peaks. Renewables are not derated; the derate hits the hours after sunset where solar cannot help.

3 · What robustness costs

Total discounted system cost 2025–2040. The climate-robust fleets cost 8–10 % more than the FEL26 Base; the air temperature rise alone is slightly cheaper because the summer-peaking demand shape replaces the winter-heavy one of the FEL26 Base.

4 · Without new thermal or nuclear capacity

Same cases, but gas, coal, oil and nuclear capacity may not exceed the FEL26 Base path. The model then builds every renewable and storage option it is allowed to and still cannot serve PJM, SERC and MISO on summer evenings: their installation ceilings for the best solar and wind classes, lithium, redox-flow, CAES and pumped hydro are used up. Transmission adds only 9 GW; its expansion is rate-capped.

Takeaways

Today's plan is short under stress. The FEL26 Base fleet loses 0.3–0.9 TWh a year in a heat-wave year, in a few hundred summer-evening hours in PJM and New York. Even the air temperature rise alone leaves a small gap every year.
Robustness is mostly firm capacity, at 8–10 % extra cost. +60–80 GW of gas turbines and CCGTs, +20–30 GW of batteries, coal kept longer; the fleets planned with the climate inputs have no shortfall.
The no-new-thermal path runs into the data potentials. With gas and nuclear capped, the binding limits are the storage and best-class renewable ceilings (lithium 37–44 GW, redox 22 GW per pool). Whether that path exists is a question about those numbers.

Not shown: the Central case (temperature-only derates, within 0.2 % of Air temp increase on every metric) and dispatch prices (a binding annual constraint leaks into the hourly duals). Caveats: one weather year per projection (2030 milder than 2018 in most pools, 2035 hotter); the thermal derate is a step from 2030 on; nuclear is derated like the other steam plants; Canada carries no thermal stress; v6-freeze data basis.

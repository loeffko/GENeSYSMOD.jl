What the cooling and heating carve-outs do to the demand peak

The load profile already contains the heating and cooling load. v6 put heating on top of it and ended up winter-peaking in seven of nine pools, which no US ISO is. The cooling split moves the peak back to summer, as it should, but overshoots the measured summer peaks by 6 to 42 %. A residual load shape for Power_General gets both right.

Checked against the ISOs

Every US ISO peaks in summer: ERCOT 85.4 GW on 20 Aug 2024, PJM 152.7 GW and ISO-NE 24.9 GW on 16 Jul 2024, MISO 122 GW on 26 Aug 2024, NYISO 29.0 GW on 8 Jul 2024, SPP 54.4 GW, CAISO 48.4 GW on 5 Sep 2024. Their peak-to-mean ratios lie between 1.62 (MISO) and 1.91 (ISO-NE).

v6 hits those magnitudes (−13 to +2 %) but in the wrong season: with 776 TWh of heating on its own winter profile on top of a load shape that already contains the heating, seven of nine pools peak in January. ERCOT's v6 winter peak of 94.6 GW compares with a measured 78 GW. That has shaped every v6 fleet so far and is a base defect in its own right.

The split fixes the season, as the question suggested, but it adds the same double count in summer. Against the scaled ISO peaks it is +6 % in PJM and MISO, +16 % in ERCOT and SPP, +22 % in New England and +42 % in New York. The cool pools suffer most because their cooling profile (max(T − 22 °C, 0)) is zero for 70 to 75 % of the hours and peaks at 25 to 28 times its mean; 8 TWh of New York cooling become a 22.8 GW spike. Real air-conditioning load peaks at roughly 10 times its mean.

The residual shape puts the base-year total on the measured FEL load shape by construction: summer peaks everywhere, −4 % (ERCOT) to +10 % (MISO), with New York (+17 %) and PJM (+13 %) high only because the FEL hourly load itself is spikier than those ISOs' 2024 load. Heating and cooling still get their own profiles, so the 2030/2035 cooling series and the different growth of cooling, heating and general demand act exactly where they should: on top of a correct base-year shape.

What this means for the climate set

  1. Residual load shape for Power_General (recommended). Derived TS_POWER_GENERAL = load shape minus the 2025 heating and cooling components, clipped at zero (8 to 42 hours per pool) and renormalised; bound through the existing Par_TagFuelToTimeseries. Data-only. Climate Base then also loses the v6 winter double count, so it differs from v6f in winter as well; that is a correction, and it has to be said in the write-up.
  2. Keep the split. Right season, but the summer peaks sit 16 % (ERCOT) to 42 % (New York) above the measured ones, exactly where the thermal stress bites, and the fleet is sized against them.
  3. Soften the cooling formula (threshold 18.3 °C or a baseline floor) keeps the double count and only trims the cool pools.

Canada is handled the same way under option 1 (its cooling returns to Power_Buildings_Cooling on a residual general shape); no model code changes in any option.
